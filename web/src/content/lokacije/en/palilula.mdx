Palilula stretches across both banks of the Danube, and that changes everything: the south is city, the north is low, damp and mostly individual builds.

## South of the Danube: Karaburma, Hadžipopovac, Višnjica

City stock, sixties to eighties. The findings are the same as anywhere from that period: a board with no RCD, a kitchen circuit overloaded by appliances added later, and here and there aluminium meeting copper without a proper connector.

The standard job is a [board rework](/usluge/consumer-units) with the circuits split.

## North: Krnjača, Borča, Ovča

Here the story is different, and worth understanding before calling anybody.

The ground is low and the water table high. That means three things a flat never deals with:

**An electrode in damp soil behaves differently.** Wet ground conducts better, so a reading can look fine in summer and poor in a dry spell. [Earth resistance](/usluge/earthing) has to be measured and read with that in mind, not taken as one number.

**Everything in the cellar is exposed.** Junction boxes, boards and sockets below ground level sit in damp nobody sees until the insulation gives. That is the most common source of the leakage current that trips an RCD after rain.

**Outdoor wiring fails faster.** Garden lighting, a well-pump feed, a garage connection — all of it needs a higher ingress rating than usually gets fitted.

For all of that, [diagnostics with measurement](/usluge/fault-diagnostics) is almost always the first step here, rather than swapping parts at random.

## Response time

Karaburma and Hadžipopovac **45 to 60 minutes**. Krnjača, Borča and Ovča at the upper end, because of the bridge.
