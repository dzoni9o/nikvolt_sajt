Novi Beograd has the most uniform housing stock in the city, and it shows in the installations. The buildings mostly went up between the sixties and the eighties to standard designs, so the same fault repeats across a whole block.

## What we find

**Boards with no RCD.** Most flats from that period were designed with ceramic fuses and no residual current device. Where the board has since been changed, the enclosure was often replaced while the same distribution logic was kept.

**A three-phase cooker on a circuit that is no longer only its own.** Kitchen wiring was designed for a cooker and not much else. With an induction hob, a microwave, a dishwasher and a coffee machine on the same circuit, tripping is not a fault — it is a consequence.

**Aluminium in the distribution.** Part of the stock is wired in aluminium. That is not a problem in itself until it meets copper without a proper connector — which is exactly what happens at every later intervention.

**Tired risers.** In some buildings the shared run from the meters to the flats is older than anything inside the flat. When measurement shows a voltage drop that does not originate in the flat, the report goes to the building manager.

## What we do most

Reworking the board — splitting circuits and adding RCD protection — is by a distance the most common job. After that come new circuits for air conditioning and induction hobs, and diagnosing intermittent tripping.

Block flats also suit [smart modules](/usluge/smart-home) well: the boxes are usually deep enough and the layouts standard, so a solution from one flat transfers easily to the next.

## Response time

Blocks, Bežanijska kosa, Fontana, Studentski grad — usually **45 to 90 minutes** from the call. Allow longer at peak times across the bridges.
