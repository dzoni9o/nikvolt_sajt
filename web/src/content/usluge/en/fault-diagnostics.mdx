An intermittent fault is the most expensive kind, because it usually gets "fixed" by swapping parts until something happens to work.

## When measurement is the only way

- **The RCD trips, but not always** — usually the water heater or a machine, but it can be moisture in outdoor wiring.
- **A breaker trips more and more often** — insulation on one circuit failing gradually.
- **Appliances keep dying** in the same spot in the flat.
- **A shock** from a tap, a sink or an appliance body.
- **Something smells of hot plastic** and you cannot see where from.
- **There has recently been work done** and something has not been right since.

## How it goes

Conversation first: when it happens, what is switched on at that moment, whether it tracks the weather or a particular appliance. This narrows things down before a single instrument comes out.

Then measurement, circuit by circuit — insulation, leakage current, loop impedance, protective conductor continuity. With the values written down, not "it looks like this one".

If the fault is inside a wall, we split the circuit in half and measure again, then again — until the section is down to a few metres. That is slow but reliable, and far cheaper than chasing walls blind.

## What you get

Numbers, not an opinion. A report with the measured values, an explanation of each, and a proposal with a price before we start anything.

If the repair happens straight away, the diagnostic fee comes off the price of the work.

---

Before you call, write down exactly when the fault appears and what is switched on at the time. That one sentence often saves an hour of measuring.
