A new installation is the one chance to do everything properly without compromise — and the one chance to bury a mistake in a wall for thirty years.

## How we work

**Layout first.** We walk the flat with you and mark what goes where: how many sockets per wall, exactly where behind the bed and behind the TV, where a double is needed, where the air conditioning feed goes and where surface-mounted lighting lands. This conversation is the cheapest part of the job and the one that pays back most.

Then the **distribution scheme** — how many circuits, what sits on each, where things are split. Kitchen and bathroom always on their own circuits; cooker, water heater and air conditioning each on theirs.

Then chasing, laying in conduit, junction boxes in accessible positions (not buried), the board, and measurements at the end.

## What we install

Copper, cross-section chosen for the circuit's load rather than out of habit. Conduit run so a cable can later be pulled out and replaced without breaking the wall open — that is the difference between an installation you maintain and one you demolish.

Junction boxes labelled and accessible. For every fault over the next twenty years, that is the difference between half an hour and half a day of searching.

## Documentation

We photograph **every wall before it is closed up**, with the routes visible. That is the document you want the first time somebody drills to hang a shelf.

Insulation, earthing and loop impedance measurements go with it, in writing.
