An LED strip is the easiest thing to install and the easiest thing to install wrongly. The difference does not show on day one; it shows after a year.

## Three things that decide its life

**Cooling.** The aluminium profile is a heatsink, not a styling choice. A strip stuck straight onto plasterboard or timber runs hotter, and roughly every 10 °C halves the life of the diodes.

**The driver.** Sized with at least 20 per cent headroom and mounted somewhere reachable — the driver is the part that fails first, and a driver buried in a ceiling means opening that ceiling.

**Feed cross-section.** Five metres of strip fed from one end through thin cable drops in voltage and shifts colour towards the far end. Feed both ends, or split it.

## What we do

We calculate watts per metre and choose the driver. We fit a profile with a diffuser — the diffuser hides the dotted look of individual diodes, which is the single thing that makes LED strip look cheap.

The feed is routed so the driver stays accessible, and we plan the control up front: a plain switch, a dimmer, or integration into a smart system if the flat already has one.

For kitchen worktops and bathrooms we pick strips with the right ingress rating — moisture and steam are what kill strips in those rooms, not electricity.
