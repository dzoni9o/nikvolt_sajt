Protection in the consumer unit does one job: cut the supply before damage happens. When it is worn out or wrongly chosen it does not do that job — and it still looks exactly the same.

## What we replace

**Breakers** that are worn, that fail to trip on a short circuit, or that are wrongly sized for the cable. The last one is more common than it should be: somebody "solved" nuisance tripping by fitting a 25 A device on a circuit designed for 16 A.

**RCDs** that fail the test button, that trip for no reason because the mechanism has aged, or that simply are not there.

**Surge protection** where it earns its place — in houses and top-floor flats, and anywhere lightning has already caused damage.

## How we choose

A breaker is chosen from **the cross-section of the cable it protects**, not from what was there before. So before replacing anything we check what is actually buried in the wall. If the circuit is 1.5 mm² aluminium, there is no case for a 16 A device on it regardless of what has been sitting there.

The RCD is chosen by application: 30 mA for personal protection, type A wherever there is electronics and inverters, selective where two-stage protection is being built.

## After fitting

We measure **the earth fault loop** — that shows whether the protection will actually disconnect fast enough — and test the RCD with both the button and an instrument. The results get written down. Without that measurement the installation is an assumption, not a job.
