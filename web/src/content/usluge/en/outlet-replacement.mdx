The outlet is the part of an installation that gets replaced most often and inspected least.

## When it needs replacing

- **Scorched or discoloured** — brown or yellow around the pin holes means the joint has been overheating.
- **Wobbles in the wall** — the box has come loose and the terminations move every time a plug is pulled.
- **The plug slides in too easily** — the contacts are slack and will heat up under load.
- **No earth in a bathroom or kitchen** — typical of buildings from before 1985.
- **Old pattern with no shutters** — in a household with small children.

## What we do

We isolate the circuit and confirm there is no voltage with an instrument, not with a neon screwdriver. We remove the old socket and **look at the state of the conductors** — this is the step that usually gets skipped. Scorched copper or aluminium at the end of the wire means the problem was never the socket.

If the wiring is aluminium the joint is made differently than for copper; mixing aluminium and copper without a proper connector is a common cause of exactly the corrosion that led to the overheating.

We fit the new socket, torque the terminals and check that it does not warm up under load.

## What goes with it

If we are replacing several outlets on one circuit we also measure **that circuit's insulation resistance**. With the power already off, that is five extra minutes, and it answers whether the problem is local or the whole circuit is near the end of its life.

For scorched outlets we suggest looking at the consumer unit too — overheating at the end of a circuit often comes with overheating at the breaker.
