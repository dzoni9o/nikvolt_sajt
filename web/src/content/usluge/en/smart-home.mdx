A smart home in an existing flat is not a rewire. It is adding modules to wiring that already works.

## What is genuinely possible without chasing

**Lighting** — a module behind the switch; the wall switch keeps working normally, plus remote control and automation.

**Blinds** — a module in the roller motor's box, with a timer and a sun sensor if wanted.

**Water heater and heating** — consumption metering and scheduled switching. This is the one item that pays for itself in a reasonable time.

**Sensors** — smoke, water leak under the washing machine, temperature, door contacts. A leak sensor beside the washing machine is one of the few things in a house that costs little and prevents expensive damage.

## What we check first

**Whether there is a neutral behind the switch.** Most modules need one. In Belgrade flats from the seventies and eighties there usually is not, so we go for a module in the junction box or a battery-powered wall button instead.

**Whether there is room in the box.** The module is small but not invisible; shallow old boxes sometimes need a deeper one.

**What the Wi-Fi coverage is like at that point.** A module buried behind a metal box in a hallway is a device that keeps dropping off the network.

## The rule we do not break

Everything has to work without internet. The wall switch turns on the light, the blind comes down, the water heater heats. Automation is an addition, not a prerequisite.
