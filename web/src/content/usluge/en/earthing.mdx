Earthing is the protection nobody notices while it works, and nobody notices has stopped working — until it shocks somebody.

## What we measure

**Earth electrode resistance** — whether the electrode in the ground is still doing anything.

**Earth fault loop impedance** — whether the protection will disconnect fast enough when a fault reaches an appliance body.

**Protective conductor continuity** per circuit — whether the wire is broken somewhere or never connected at all. This is the most common finding in flats where somebody has "tidied up" the installation.

All with a calibrated instrument. The result is a record with numbers, not an estimate.

## Three states we find

**The wire is there and connected to nothing.** Classic in flats where a third wire was added later, reached the first junction box and stopped.

**Connected, but the joint has corroded.** Measured resistance is high; protection works more slowly than it should, or not at all.

**Everything connected, but the electrode is finished.** In older houses, where the tape in the ground lost contact long ago.

## Why this is not a formality

In an installation without sound earthing, **an RCD will not respond** to a fault on an appliance body until somebody touches it. The device is there, it looks like protection, and it protects nothing. That is why earthing gets measured before any other conclusion is drawn about an installation's safety.

There is more on the symptoms in the article on [recognising poor grounding](/blog/grounding-check).

## Do not put it off

If something has already given you a shock at the sink or the tap, do not put it off — the measurement is one visit and gets done the same day.
