Grounding is the part of the installation everyone forgets until something goes wrong. Then it&rsquo;s the only thing they think about.

## What &ldquo;poor grounding&rdquo; actually means

A safe installation has a **low-resistance path** from any exposed metal back to the source. &ldquo;Poor grounding&rdquo; means that path is either too high in resistance, broken somewhere along the way, or never installed.

## Symptoms you can notice without a meter

- A faint **tingle** when touching a metal appliance (washing machine, oven, boiler).
- The RCD trips on a circuit that has no obvious leakage.
- Outlets in older parts of the building have only **two pins** of contact.
- Network and audio gear hums noticeably.

## Symptoms that need a meter

- Loop impedance higher than expected for the breaker size.
- Earth resistance above 10 Ω at the rod (in most domestic setups it should be a few ohms).
- Floating neutral — voltage between earth and neutral that isn&rsquo;t close to zero.

## How we measure it

We use a calibrated **earth resistance tester** with the spike-and-stake method, plus loop impedance from each circuit. Test takes about 30 minutes for an apartment, longer for a house.

## When you need to act

If you feel a tingle, stop using the appliance and call. That&rsquo;s the one symptom that crosses from &ldquo;inconvenience&rdquo; to &ldquo;risk&rdquo; without warning.
