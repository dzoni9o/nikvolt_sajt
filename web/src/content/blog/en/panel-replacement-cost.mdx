One of the questions we get most often is: **what does it cost to replace a consumer unit?** The answer depends on several things, and this article breaks all of them down so you know what you are paying for and why.

> **Note:** These are indicative and depend on the number of circuits, the grade of materials and the state of the existing installation. The figures are final: we are not registered for VAT, so no tax is added on top.

## What drives the price

### 1. Number of circuits

Every circuit — lighting, sockets, cooker, washing machine, air conditioning — needs its own breaker. More circuits means a bigger board and a higher price.

### 2. Grade of materials

The gap between a cheap board and a good one can be 15,000–30,000 RSD. We recommend Hager, Schneider Electric or ABB — established brands with a warranty behind them.

### 3. State of the existing installation

If the wiring is old and parts of it have to be replaced along with the board, the price rises. This only becomes clear on inspection.

### 4. Accessibility

Boards in cellars, service shafts or awkward positions take more work to reach and to wire.

## What is included

Every board replacement we do covers:

- removing the old board
- fitting the new board with all its components
- labelling every circuit permanently
- insulation and RCD testing on completion
- **photo documentation** of the finished work
- a warranty on the work carried out

## Why the cheapest quote is not the cheapest

The consumer unit is the heart of a flat's installation. A badly built one causes:

- fire from overheating at poor joints
- damage to expensive electronics from unstable supply
- problems when selling the flat, where evidence of condition is asked for

Always ask for a quote that includes **measurements on completion** — that is the only way to know the job was done properly.
